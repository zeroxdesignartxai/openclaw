# Draft Report to DFPI Regarding Auto Financing, Repossession, and Related Conduct

## Audience

California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI)

## Scope

This report summarizes documented concerns involving the sale and financing of a used 2017 Honda Civic, subsequent servicing and transfer of the loan, repossession conduct, insurance-related charges, credit reporting issues, and potential disclosure violations involving Karplus Warehouse, Inc. / Karplus Auto Sales, Green Road Finance Inc., On Time Financing Inc., and the repossession company identified in the record as Reign Recovery.

## Executive Summary

The document set reflects a pattern of alleged nondisclosure, unexplained charges, inconsistent lender/servicer information, disputed add-on products, possible improper credit reporting, and repossession-related misconduct. The strongest supported issues are:

- alleged failure to clearly disclose contract terms and optional add-on products at sale;
- alleged undisclosed tracking or starter-interrupt technology;
- conflicting lender/servicer history involving Karplus, On Time Financing, and Green Road Finance;
- disputed down-payment amounts and questions about how payments were applied;
- variable insurance charges and confusion over GAP coverage;
- repossession conduct involving changing fees, lack of clear notice, refusal to promptly release the vehicle, and alleged vehicle damage.

The file set also contains a prior CFPB complaint and an existing DFPI complaint concerning Green Road Finance. A DFPI response dated November 12, 2024 states the complaint could not proceed directly without the consumer's consent to disclose personal information to Green Road Finance.

This report uses the available files as evidence of allegations and documented transactions. It does not conclude that fraud is proven, but the records do show multiple fraud and regulatory-risk indicators that warrant DFPI review.

## Key Findings

### 1. Possible deceptive or incomplete sales disclosures at origination

The file `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf` states that when the vehicle was purchased, the buyer was directed where to sign without a meaningful explanation of contract terms, and was told that several add-ons were required, including a tracking device, window etching, and an extended warranty. The same file states the buyer was told there was no prepayment penalty and that the vehicle had been a rental, but no meaningful detail was provided.

Fraud / violation indicators:

- optional products may have been presented as mandatory;
- financing terms may not have been adequately explained before signing;
- oral statements about prepayment may have conflicted with written contract terms;
- rental-use history may have been incompletely disclosed.

Evidence:

- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`
- `Car Events - Google Docs.docx`
- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`

### 2. Disputed down payment and unexplained optional charges

The CFPB complaint states the contract showed a $6,000 down payment while receipts reflected $5,600, creating a documented discrepancy of $400. The same complaint identifies optional line items that the consumer says were not expressly authorized, including:

- Theft Deterrent Device: $695
- Surface Protection Product: $1,495

The narrative file separately states that $4,600 was paid on July 6, 2020, an additional $1,000 on July 11, 2020, and $550.36 in August 2020.

Fraud / violation indicators:

- inaccurate disclosure of down payment;
- possible charging for optional products without informed consent;
- possible inflation of financed amount through add-on products.

Evidence:

- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`
- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`
- `Car Events - Google Docs.docx`

### 3. Undisclosed tracking / starter-interrupt device concerns

The records repeatedly state the buyer asked to remove the tracking device but was told it was required. The CFPB complaint specifically cites concern that electronic tracking or starter-interrupt technology was not properly disclosed and that written consent may not have been obtained.

Fraud / violation indicators:

- possible failure to disclose installation and terms of electronic tracking or starter-interrupt technology;
- possible use of coercive sales tactics by stating the product was mandatory.

Evidence:

- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`
- `Car Events - Google Docs.docx`
- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`

### 4. Lender-transfer confusion and possible servicing/reporting inaccuracies

The file set describes the loan as originating through the dealership and then being associated with On Time Financing and Green Road Finance. The CFPB complaint states the credit report listed Green Road as the initial lender and then On Time, which the consumer says was inaccurate because Karplus was the originating seller and the lender sequence was misrepresented.

The same complaint states that On Time ran credit without permission after the loan had already been sold. That allegation is serious but is not independently verified in the current file set beyond the complaint narrative.

Fraud / violation indicators:

- inaccurate furnishing or reporting of lender history;
- poor or misleading transfer disclosures;
- potential unauthorized credit inquiry if supported by credit file records.

Evidence:

- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`
- `Experian1.pdf`
- `Experian GreenRoad.pdf`
- `Gmail - Request for Clarification and resolution of auto Loan Transfer Issues.pdf`
- `Gmail - Request for Clarification on Loan Terms and Payment Discrepancies.pdf`

### 5. Possible unlicensed or improperly documented collection/servicing activity by On Time Financing

The file `OnTime Notes.txt` states that California required licensure by January 2020 for companies providing financial products and services, and asserts that On Time Financing Inc. was not licensed by DFPI and had never been registered. That note is useful as a lead, but by itself it is not the same as an official licensing record.

The same note states On Time billing statements reflected incorrect payment dates and amounts.

Regulatory-risk indicators:

- possible servicing or collection activity by an entity whose licensing status should be independently verified;
- possible inaccurate billing statements.

Evidence:

- `OnTime Notes.txt`
- `11-2020 OnTime Finance Payment.pdf`
- `12-2020 OnTime Finance Paymenyt.pdf`
- `01-2021 OnTime Finance .pdf`
- `02-2021 OnTime Finance Payment.pdf`
- `8-2020 OnTime Finance Payment.pdf`
- `9-2020 OnTime Finance Payment.pdf`
- `10-2020 OnTime Finance Payment.pdf`

Open verification needed:

- confirm On Time Financing Inc.'s license status during the exact servicing period using DFPI or NMLS records;
- compare billing statements to bank/payment proof and contract terms.

### 6. Green Road Finance appears licensed as a DFPI debt collector, but the records still show multiple disputed practices

The DFPI licensing file identifies Green Road Finance Inc. as a regulated entity under debt collectors, License No. `10423-99`, originally licensed on March 6, 2023. The NMLS company report likewise reflects California DFPI debt collection licensing.

This matters because the file set still attributes to Green Road:

- confusing responses about GAP insurance;
- disputed principal reduction and unexplained charges;
- requests for payment via unusual channels such as PayNearMe;
- correspondence identifying itself as from a debt collector;
- alleged failure to provide clear notice or account breakdowns.

Evidence:

- `DFPI Licenseing and Info.pdf`
- `NMLS Geen Road Report  - Company.pdf`
- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`

### 7. Insurance and GAP-related confusion

The CFPB complaint and narrative files describe repeated problems involving insurance and GAP:

- the consumer says Green Road and On Time each redirected responsibility for GAP coverage;
- Green Road allegedly directed the consumer back to Karplus to redeem GAP;
- insurance charges allegedly varied over time;
- the consumer alleges charges were imposed during a brief period while insurance was pending;
- the lender allegedly selected the deductible, causing delays.

The warranty claim summary in `Portfolio Claim Summery (Sheldon).pdf` shows one approved claim for $589.08 opened on November 11, 2020, which supports the existence of at least one service-contract/warranty claim but does not by itself resolve whether the warranty/GAP products were properly sold or administered.

Fraud / violation indicators:

- possible failure to clearly identify who administered GAP or related protection products;
- possible force-placed or otherwise unexplained insurance-related charges;
- possible misrepresentation of optional-product benefits.

Evidence:

- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`
- `Portfolio Claim Summery (Sheldon).pdf`
- `Gmail - Request for Clarification on Loan Terms and Payment Discrepancies.pdf`

### 8. Repossession conduct raises notice, fee, access, and damage concerns

The narrative files state the vehicle was repossessed around 2 to 3 AM without prior notice, that the repossession team first photographed the vehicle and later returned with a tow truck, and that the car was dragged in a manner inconsistent with the owner's manual requirement for flatbed towing. The records also state:

- the repossession company refused to release the car even when funds were available;
- fees and payment demands changed repeatedly;
- cash or unusual payment methods were demanded;
- the consumer had difficulty obtaining the correct contact information;
- the repossession company allegedly damaged the vehicle during repossession;
- the repossession company allegedly had a key copy that had never been disclosed to the buyer.

An April 13, 2023 email to Parker Stanbury also states that no default correspondence or tow-company notice was received, and identifies an amount sought of $1,386.36 in connection with Green Road Finance.

Supporting transaction records include the Lyft receipt from April 27, 2023 for rides related to recovering the vehicle, and the email regarding a car-seat claim.

Fraud / violation indicators:

- potentially improper repossession notice and release procedures;
- fee inflation or inconsistent fee disclosure;
- possible improper possession or use of duplicate keys;
- physical damage to collateral during repossession;
- possible interference with retrieval of personal property.

Evidence:

- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`
- `Car Events - Google Docs.docx`
- `Gmail -Complaint Info Request CA1-FRJ-C2R.pdf`
- `lyft recipt to pick up car April 27-2023.pdf`
- `Gmail - Recipt for car seat claim20003834029.pdf`
- `Repo Payment Wells Fargo Branch Transaction Record.pdf`

### 9. Extensive post-sale repair history supports concern about vehicle condition, but does not alone prove misrepresentation

The file set contains numerous auto-parts receipts and repair records from 2020 through 2024, including O'Reilly, Walmart, AutoZone, Amazon, Roy's Auto Repair, Sheldon Auto Care, and Steve Nie Auto Repair. These records support the claim that the vehicle required repeated repairs and substantial out-of-pocket expense after purchase.

This evidence supports consumer harm and vehicle-condition concerns. Standing alone, it does not prove title washing, VIN washing, or dealership knowledge of hidden defects, but it does strengthen the case for further review of vehicle history disclosures and sale-condition representations.

Evidence:

- multiple files under `Auto Repair Recipts`
- `RoysAutoRepair6-1-21.pdf`
- `Sheldon Auto Care Recipt - Wells Fargo .pdf`
- `Steve Nie Auto Repair Recipt8-2-24.pdf`

## Risk / Exposure Areas for DFPI Review

Based on the file set, the most significant issues for DFPI review are:

- deceptive or incomplete disclosures at the time of sale and financing;
- optional add-on products potentially sold as mandatory;
- disputed payment application and principal balance calculations;
- lender-transfer and credit-reporting irregularities;
- possible improper insurance and GAP administration;
- repossession practices, fee changes, notice problems, and collateral damage;
- possible licensing or billing irregularities involving On Time Financing;
- debt-collection conduct by Green Road Finance after transfer of the account.
