# Draft Report to the FTC Regarding Deceptive Auto Sale, Financing, and Repossession Practices

## Audience

Federal Trade Commission (FTC)

## Scope

This report summarizes evidence and allegations concerning the sale, financing, servicing, and repossession of a used 2017 Honda Civic involving Karplus Warehouse, Inc. / Karplus Auto Sales, Green Road Finance Inc., On Time Financing Inc., and the repossession company identified in the records as Reign Recovery.

## Executive Summary

The file set reflects a pattern of alleged deceptive and unfair conduct in connection with a used-car sale and related financing. The strongest FTC-relevant issues are:

- optional add-on products allegedly presented as mandatory;
- contract terms allegedly not explained before signing;
- possible misleading statements about prepayment and financing terms;
- disputed down-payment and account-balance calculations;
- possible undisclosed tracking or starter-interrupt technology;
- lender-transfer confusion and possible credit-reporting irregularities;
- repossession-related fee changes, communication failures, and alleged vehicle damage.

The documents support a request for FTC review of possible unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the marketing and servicing of this auto transaction.

## Key Findings

### 1. Possible deceptive sale process

The file `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf` states the buyer returned with more money and found the contract already prepared, was directed where to sign, and did not receive a meaningful explanation of the terms. The same file states the buyer was told certain products were required, including:

- tracking device;
- window etching;
- extended warranty.

The buyer also states they were told there was no prepayment penalty.

FTC concern:

- deceptive or unfair sales tactics if optional products were represented as mandatory or if material terms were not adequately disclosed before execution.

Evidence:

- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`
- `Car Events - Google Docs.docx`
- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`

### 2. Disputed optional add-ons and down-payment accounting

The CFPB complaint states the contract showed a $6,000 down payment while receipts reflected $5,600. The same complaint identifies line items the consumer states were not expressly authorized:

- Theft Deterrent Device: $695
- Surface Protection Product: $1,495

FTC concern:

- misrepresentation of optional product charges;
- deceptive inflation of financed amounts;
- inaccurate transaction disclosures.

Evidence:

- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`
- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`

### 3. Possible undisclosed electronic tracking / starter interrupt technology

The records state the buyer asked about removing a tracking device and was told it was required. The CFPB complaint states the consumer did not receive proper disclosure regarding tracking or starter-interrupt technology.

FTC concern:

- failure to clearly disclose material product features affecting use, location tracking, or ability to operate the vehicle;
- unfair coercion if a tracking product was treated as non-optional without proper disclosure.

Evidence:

- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`
- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`

### 4. Confusing lender / servicer sequence

The records indicate the account involved Karplus, On Time Financing, and Green Road Finance. The consumer states that the tradeline history and lender sequence shown on credit reporting were inaccurate and that On Time may have run credit after the account had already transferred.

FTC concern:

- misleading loan-transfer communications;
- deceptive account servicing practices;
- inaccurate furnishing or use of consumer information.

Evidence:

- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`
- `Experian1.pdf`
- `Experian GreenRoad.pdf`

### 5. Insurance and GAP confusion

The complaint records state that On Time and Green Road redirected responsibility for GAP coverage, and that Green Road directed the consumer back to Karplus to redeem GAP. The consumer also alleges varying insurance charges and confusion over approval and deductible handling.

FTC concern:

- misleading or unclear administration of ancillary products sold with financing;
- possible deceptive billing of insurance-related charges.

Evidence:

- `CFPD Complaint Detail.pdf`
- `Portfolio Claim Summery (Sheldon).pdf`

### 6. Repossession-related unfairness

The records state the vehicle was repossessed in the early morning, the release requirements and fees changed repeatedly, the repossession company was difficult to reach, and the vehicle was allegedly damaged during repossession. An April 13, 2023 email also states the consumer did not receive clear notice of default or proper repossession contact information.

FTC concern:

- unfair post-default conduct;
- misleading fee demands;
- damage to consumer property in connection with collection activity.

Evidence:

- `Car Events - Google Docs.pdf`
- `Gmail -Complaint Info Request CA1-FRJ-C2R.pdf`
- `lyft recipt to pick up car April 27-2023.pdf`
- `Gmail - Recipt for car seat claim20003834029.pdf`

## Consumer Harm

The records reflect the following harm:

- substantial upfront payments and disputed loan accounting;
- repeated out-of-pocket repair expenses over several years;
- repossession-related transportation and recovery costs;
- alleged damage to the vehicle;
- disruption and distress caused by repossession and servicing confusion.

## Requested FTC Action

I request the FTC review whether the sale, financing, ancillary product disclosures, servicing, and repossession conduct in this matter involved unfair or deceptive practices. Specifically, I request review of:

- whether optional products were sold as mandatory;
- whether material finance terms were clearly disclosed;
- whether tracking or starter-interrupt technology was disclosed and consented to;
- whether down-payment, fees, and insurance charges were accurately represented;
- whether post-sale and post-transfer servicing communications were misleading;
- whether repossession-related conduct involved unfair or deceptive practices.

## Short FTC Complaint Paragraph

I am reporting a used-car sale and financing transaction that appears to involve deceptive add-on sales, inadequate contract disclosure, disputed down-payment and fee accounting, possible undisclosed tracking technology, confusing loan-transfer and credit-reporting history, and unfair repossession practices. My records show that optional products may have been presented as mandatory, financing terms may not have been clearly explained, insurance and GAP responsibilities were repeatedly shifted between companies, and repossession fees and release requirements changed while the vehicle was allegedly damaged. I request FTC review of possible unfair or deceptive acts or practices involving the dealership, finance companies, and repossession-related conduct.
